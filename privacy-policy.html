NoMedia Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 October 2026

This policy is for NoMedia on Safari, on Mac and on iPhone or iPad. NoMedia hides images and videos on websites you choose. The developer of NoMedia does not run a server for this extension and does not receive your browsing data.

What stays on your device

NoMedia stores your settings in Safari's own storage on your device. That includes:

  • Whether the extension is on, and which kinds of media it hides (images, videos, background images, and drawn logos).
  • The websites you add to your block list or allow list, and whether each site covers photos, videos, or both.
  • Display choices, such as blur, placeholder, or removed, and whether a click on the toolbar icon opens the menu or toggles the current site.
  • Permanent choices to block or allow one image or video. Those stay on that device and are not synced.
  • A temporary note that you asked to show media on the current page. That note lasts until you leave the page or close the tab, and it is not synced.

If iCloud is on for Safari, Safari may sync the site lists and the other settings above, except the permanent per-image and per-video choices and the temporary page note, to your other Apple devices where this extension is installed. That sync is provided by Apple, not by the developer of NoMedia. The developer cannot see it. These settings stay separate from NoMedia on Chrome, Firefox, and Edge.

You can also save your settings to a file and restore that file later. That file stays wherever you save it. NoMedia does not upload it.

What the extension sees on a page

To hide pictures and videos, NoMedia looks at the page you are viewing and changes how images, videos, and some drawn graphics are displayed. If you turn on "Stop downloads on blocked sites," or you permanently block or allow one image or video file, NoMedia tells Safari which image and video requests to block or allow. Those rules are built on your device from your own choices.

This happens entirely in your browser. Page contents, addresses you visit, and the files a page requests are not sent to the developer or to any other party by NoMedia. They are not saved as a browsing history. Choosing one picture or video, by a right-click on a Mac or a finger-hold on iPhone or iPad, uses that spot on the page only for that moment, and that position is not stored. A permanent block or allow saves that file's address on the device.

Private Browsing and local files

NoMedia does not run in Private Browsing until you allow it. Open Safari Settings, go to Extensions, select NoMedia, and turn on Private Browsing. NoMedia does not change files you open from your device. Safari does not let an extension change those pages.

What NoMedia does not do

  • It does not collect your name, email address, contacts, location, health information, or payment information.
  • It does not read passwords, messages, or form contents in order to keep or share them.
  • It does not include advertising or analytics.
  • It does not sell or transfer user data to anyone.
  • It does not use data for purposes unrelated to hiding and revealing media.
  • It does not use data to determine creditworthiness or for lending.
  • It does not download or run code from the internet. All of its code is inside the app you install.

How long information is kept

Settings remain in Safari until you change them, use "Reset to defaults" in the extension's settings, or uninstall NoMedia. Uninstalling removes the extension's stored settings from that device. Temporary "show media on this page" choices end when you navigate away or close the tab.

Children

NoMedia is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this same address and the date at the top will be updated. If a change affects how the extension handles data, it will also be described in the app's update notes on the App Store.
